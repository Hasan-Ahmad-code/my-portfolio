Abstract

I'm a final-year Computer Science student and Research Associate at SINES, NUST, working on retrieval-augmented generation for scientific literature. A question keeps surfacing in my work: when should a language model trust what it retrieves, and when should it not? I build RAG systems from scratch, stress-test them under noisy retrieval, and write up what the results actually show.

Keywords — retrieval-augmented generation, trustworthy & evidence-grounded AI, human-AI interaction, sustainable & efficient computing, natural language processing

Research focus

My current survey covers 30+ papers across six themes in dynamic memory and LLM adaptation.

Dynamic memory

How LLMs store and retrieve information within and across contexts, including KV-cache and external memory.

Test-time adaptation

Techniques that let models adapt to new distributions at inference time — without retraining.

Adaptive context

Deciding when to trust retrieved context over parametric knowledge — a key challenge in RAG and beyond.
